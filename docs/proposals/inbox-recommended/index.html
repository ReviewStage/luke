A recommended option you can see

Dean: "The 'Recommended' badge on Inbox items isn't prominent enough. Let's see some proposed designs on a show." Every frame below is main's real desktop stylesheet (built from 79d807e8), light and dark, over the DOM the real InboxPane drew for synthetic questions. Each option adds only the CSS shown under its heading. Frames are 600px wide where the item stands beside its list and 400px wide in the narrow tier, where it stands alone under Back.

Pick: (a), a small filled "Recommended" badge at the option's top right recommended

The badge takes the geometry of the app's own badges (Experimental, Read-only: 10.5px, 560 weight, a 16px line, a 4px corner) and the accent fill of the primary button and the focus ring, with white words at 4.7:1 in both themes. It stands in one place whatever the label's length, so it reads at a glance in the narrow tier, where today's "· Recommended" wraps to its own line. Once the item is answered it steps back to the quiet grey badge, so the thread shows what was recommended without asking for anything.

The options keep the agent's order and nothing is pre-selected: a press is still the one way to answer. It is one small desktop PR (Build).

Today

The recommendation is the word "Recommended" after a middle dot, in the muted ink every option's detail uses, at the end of the label's line. When the label is long it wraps to the second line (narrow tier), and in a list of four it reads like the details around it. Once answered it stays the same muted words.

Answered

Found while drawing the states: on main the keyboard's ring on an option is clipped at the left and bottom edges, because the message body that holds the options is overflow: hidden. It is visible in every focus frame below, so the build fixes it in passing (Build).

Research

The full notes, with quotes, are in docs/proposals/inbox-recommended/research.md. In short:

ProductHow it marks the recommended choice
LinearTriage suggestions sit in a pending state of their own under the title, each accepted or declined, with the reason on hover. No badge. docs
GitHubSuggested reviewers are ordinary rows with an inline "Request". Recommended workflow templates are a group shown first. Primer's Label is a small word-shaped label, and its accessibility notes say colour alone must not carry the meaning. reviewers, templates, Label, accessibility
StripeThe pricing table lets a merchant highlight one product among up to four cards, styled by the merchant's theme. docs
VercelA small "Popular" pill on Pro's title line, and Pro's button is the only filled one. pricing
Claude CodeAskUserQuestion puts the recommended option first and appends "(Recommended)" to its label. No colour. the tool's prompt, quoted
ChatGPT"Which response do you prefer?" marks neither reply on purpose, because the unprompted choice is the signal. forum
macOSThe default button is filled with the accent and answers Return. It is never destructive, and an alert that needs reading should have no default. Buttons, Alerts
RaycastThe primary action is first in the Action Panel and runs on ↵. The ↵ glyph is the only mark. manual, API
AtlassianThe Lozenge: one or two words, filled by default, and "don't use color alone". docs

What carries over: say it in a word or two on the option's own title line, never by colour alone. Keep a ring and tint for a few cards compared side by side. Tie a default to Return only where acting without reading is safe, which an answer an agent will act on is not.

(a) A filled badge at the top right pick

The label and the badge share the option's first line, with the badge pushed to the right edge. Hover, focus and pressed are the option's own, unchanged. Answered, the badge goes to the quiet grey of the Read-only badge.

  • For: it is the clearest of the four at a glance, it stays in one place in every width, it is a word rather than a colour, and it holds AA in both themes. It reuses a shape the app already has, and the option's selection look stays the only selection look.
  • Against: it is the one accent fill on the pane. That is deliberate, and the Send button is ink, not accent, so they don't compete. A long label wraps a little earlier to leave the badge its room.

(b) An accent ring and tint, with a tinted badge

The recommended card wears a faint accent ground and an accent hairline, a step stronger on hover, with a tinted badge at its top right. Picked, its ring goes to full accent. Answered, the ring and tint go and the badge goes quiet.

  • For: the loudest at rest, and the badge's ink is the primary ink, so it reads at 12:1 or better.
  • Against: it spends the selection's own language. A ring is what "picked" looks like, so in "Another picked" two cards are ringed and you have to read to see which one you chose, and the recommended card picked looks nearly the same as at rest. This is the pricing-table pattern, made for three plans side by side. Here it sits in a list of answers.

(c) A sparkle and the word, leading the label

A sparkle and "Recommended" in the accent ink lead the label on its line. Answered, they go to the muted ink.

(d) Listed first, with an eyebrow, optionally pre-selected

The recommended option moves to the top, with "Recommended" in small accent ink over its label. The pre-selected form below also presses it and gives it the keyboard as the item opens, so Send is ready and Return accepts it.

  • For: it follows Claude Code's and Raycast's convention, and pre-selected it makes the recommended answer one keystroke.
  • Against: it reorders what the agent wrote, and an agent's context blocks may refer to "the second option" or lay the options out from least to most change. The eyebrow is the accent as small ink, with the same contrast shortfall as (c). Pre-selection turns Send into "accept without reading" on an answer the agent will act on, which the HIG warns against for anything that needs reading. It also gives every choice a draft before you have touched it, and nothing to pre-select in a choice that allows several.

Build

Size: one small desktop PR, about 40 lines of TSX and CSS plus a test. Commit feat(desktop): an Inbox option the agent recommends wears a Recommended badge.

  • apps/desktop/src/renderer/planning/inbox-pane.tsx: ChoiceOptions and Proposal draw an option's head as one shared component, the label then the badge, with the badge settled in Proposal. The badge is a span with the words "Recommended", so a screen reader reads it after the label as it does today.
  • apps/desktop/src/renderer/styles/planning.css: .inbox-option-head and .inbox-recommended as shown under (a), beside .inbox-option.
  • The badge lives entirely inside the option element (.inbox-option-head in the button or li), with no dependence on the pane's width or on a wrapper only InboxPane has. The Inbox narrow-tier redesign's list cards, which draw ChoiceOptions and Proposal too, get it unchanged.
  • Separately, the clipped focus ring: the options' message body clips with overflow: clip and overflow-clip-margin: var(--focus-ring-reach), as the pane's crumbs already do (.inbox-crumbs), so the ring shows whole and long words still don't spill.
  • Test: a new inbox-pane.test.ts renders an open and an answered item and asserts that the badge stands on the recommended option alone, that an item without a recommendation draws none, and that once answered the pick and the recommendation are both said when they differ.
  • Unchanged: the agent's own record of what it asked (tool-call-model.ts's "(recommended)" text line) and the wire.

Decisions for Dean

  1. Pre-select the recommended option? Recommended: no. An Inbox answer is acted on by an agent, so it should be a deliberate press, and the HIG leaves no default where people need to read first. A choice that allows several has nothing to pre-select.
  2. Reorder it first? Recommended: no in the window, since the agent's order may carry meaning. If you want first-position as well, the cheaper way is a clause in the inbox_post tool's description (packages/service/src/agents/coder-host/inbox.ts) asking agents to list the recommended option first, which is a separate one-line service PR.
  3. Once answered, quiet or still accent? Recommended: quiet. The answer is settled, and the pick's ring and "Your pick" are what the eye should land on.
